## Weapon: Chain Whip
### Data Extract Profile

Appearing primarily on the arenas of [[Solaris VII]], the '''Chain Whip''' is a [[BattleMech|BattleMech scale]] [[Melee Weapon]].

==Description==
The Chain Whip was made to entangle enemy 'Mechs. Tested in the [[Word of Blake]] assault on Solaris, the first iteration used pure myomers and wasn't effective. The Blakists made it work by adding additional armor alloy chains to reinforce the myomers. This version of the Chain Whip worked well. Despite its limited damage, the Blakists found it useful for immobilizing enemy forces.<ref name=to288>''Tactical Operations'', p. 288</ref>

## Weapon: Claw
### Data Extract Profile
{{distinguish|text=the [[Battle Claw|Battle Armor version of the same]]}}

[[File:BattleMech Claw.jpg|190px|right|thumb|BattleMech ''Claw'']]

==Description==
Introduced in [[3060]] by the [[Lyran Alliance]], these are specially modified [[BattleMech]] hand actuators, intended to rend armor in close-quarters combat. Due to the strength needed for its function, claws are less dexterous than standard hand actuators, making them a poor choice to do anything else.<ref name=TO_AUaE>{{cite book|title=Tactical Operations: Advanced Units & Equipment|edition=6th|page=101}}</ref>

Their popularity with both [[Arena Gladiator|gladiators]] and crowds on various [[:Category:Game Worlds|game world]] arenas, prompted numerous redesigns until [[Solaris Arms]] of [[Solaris]] set the final standard in the early 3060s on their ''[[Mantis (BattleMech)|Mantis]]'' dueling 'Mech.
These Battlemech Claws seen on the various game worlds also had the unintended effect of possibly inspiring the Battlemech [[Talon (weapon)|Talons]] created and used by [[Clan Jade Falcon]].<ref name=TO_AUaE/>

==Notes==
* Full game rules for BattleMech claws can be found in ''[[Tactical Operations: Advanced Units & Equipment]]'', where as other rulebooks may list them as physical weapons.

The '''Dual Saw''' is a piece of construction equipment that is rarely seen on the battlefield. Though it has been used by the arena fighters on [[Solaris]],<ref name="MP:SVIIp55">''MapPack: Solaris VII'', p. 55: "New Equipment - Dual Saw"</ref> the lack of range and interference with other equipment mean it is not popular with the regular armies of the Inner Sphere. ## Weapon: Flail
### Data Extract Profile
[[file:mechflail.png|right|100px|thumb|BattleMech Flail]]

==Description==
The [[BattleMech]] '''Flail''' is a physical attack weapon developed in the arenas of [[Solaris VII]]. Though more controllable than the [[Wrecking Ball]] that inspired it, the Flail is still tricky to use. [[MechWarrior (pilot)|MechWarriors]] have been known to inflict damage on their own 'Mechs from the swinging Flail head. It is possible that the swinging Flail will cause the 'Mech using it to overbalance, bringing the 'Mech down at an inopportune moment.

==Rules==
<div class="gamerules">
===Game Rules===
The BattleMech Flail inflicts 9 points of damage on a successful to hit roll. It isn't affected by tonnage or [[triple-strength myomer]]. As it is so unwieldy, there is a 1 point penalty on to hit rolls. If the Flail user rolls a 2 on the to-hit roll, the miss inflicts 5 points of damage on the Flail-wielding 'Mech's front side and the MechWarrior must make a piloting skill roll to avoid falling down.
</div>

## Weapon: Lance (weapon)
### Data Extract Profile
[[File:BattleMech Lance.jpg|right|thumb|BattleMech Lance]]

==Description==
The [[BattleMech]] '''Lance''' is a special melee weapon designed to pierce armor plating. Unlike the medieval weapon of the same name, the Lance is a long, thin spike that juts out from a 'Mech's arm; it is not a large spear-like weapon carried in the hand. In combat the Lance pierces the armor plating of an enemy unit, possibly damaging or destroying internal systems.

Though initially used in the [[Solaris Games]], it was put into full production as of 3064.

==Rules==
<div class="gamerules">
===Game Rules===
In game terms, the Lance can be mounted by BattleMechs and IndustrialMechs in the arms. Each arm can carry one Lance. The Lance requires a single critical slot and weighs one ton for every twenty tons the 'Mech weighs. It attacks with a +1 penalty, and inflicts one point of damage for every five tons the 'Mech weighs. In addition, if armor remains after a Lance attack, the attacker should roll 2D6. On 10+ a single point of damage is applied to the internal structure. Roll for a critical hit at a -2 modifier. A Lance that hits an unarmored location has the usual chance for inflicting critical hits.

A Lance's battle value is equal to the damage it inflicts.
</div>

## Weapon: Ram Plate
### Data Extract Profile
==Description==
The '''Ram Plate''' is a melee weapon that appeared during the [[Hanseatic Crusade]] in 3140, notably being a key feature on the [[Rhino]] Quad 'Mech.

===Handling===
Similar to weapons-grade [[Spikes]], the Ram Plate both increases the 'mech's damage during a charge, and reduces the damage received in return.  Unlike the the general-melee use Spikes, the Ram Plate modifies both by an impressive 50%, though only in relation to Charge attacks.

Due to the solid nature of the plate, there are stringent mounting limitations.  The Ram Plate can only be mounted on a Quad 'mech, and furthermore one with [[Reinforced Structure]].<ref name=OTPHCp8-10>''Operational Turning Points: Hanseatic Crusade'', pp. 30</ref>  While a potentially destructive melee option, the mounting restrictions - as well as the risks associated with a full-body charge - has resulted in virtually zero adoption of the Ram Plate outside of its initial use on the Rhino.

## Weapon: Retractable Blade
### Data Extract Profile
The '''Retractable Blade''', introduced circa [[2420]], is a [[Melee Weapon|melee weapon]] used by [[BattleMech]]s.  

==History==
Originally designed by the [[Terran Hegemony]] for use with [[AgroMech]]s, the Retractable Blade was implemented by the [[Word of Blake]] for their [[Celestial series]] [[OmniMech]]s. During the [[Jihad]], the technology was dispersed throughout the [[Inner Sphere]]. 

==Rules==
<div class="gamerules">
===Game Rules===
The Retractable Blade weighs one ton per every twenty tons the 'Mech weighs, rounded up, plus 0.5 tons. The blade takes up a number of critical spots equal to one per every twenty tons the 'Mech weighs, rounded up, plus one. The blade does one point of damage for every ten tons the 'Mech weighs, rounded up.

===Dark Age Game Rules===
In MechWarrior: Age of Destruction, it is considered Heavy Gear and costs twenty-two points. It attaches to "Damage". As an option, when this unit succeeds at a close combat attack using its primary damage value, it gets +1 to its primary damage value for this attack.

===Tactical Operations: Advanced Rules===
When punching with a retracted blade the user can choose to extend the blade; with the chance to score a critical on the location but also with the risk of breaking the blade. </div>

## Weapon: Spikes
### Data Extract Profile
[[File:BattleMech Spikes.jpg|190px|right|thumb|BattleMech Spikes]]

==Description==
'''Spikes''' have been used as decoration on [[BattleMech]]s for centuries, but had no effect in combat. The [[Federated Commonwealth]] changed that in [[3051]] when it introduced reinforced and strengthened Spikes capable of inflicting damage on enemy units in close combat. Based on the same engineering reports that gave birth to the [[Lyran Commonwealth]]'s [[Hatchet]], these [[Experimental Technology|experimental]] melee weapons have demonstrated their effectiveness in the [[Solaris]] arenas.

===Handling===
The Spikes used in the arenas serve two purposes: 
# They absorb some of the damage from an opponent's physical attacks.
# They inflict additional damage when performing physical attacks.

In the up-close and personal fighting style most arena warriors employ, Spikes become very dangerous to their enemies. The only drawback to the combat ready Spikes is the increased weight and space they consume when compared to their decorative counterparts. Each cluster of Spikes takes up as much room as a [[Small Laser]]. If a warrior isn't going to engage in melee combat, then the Spikes become useless.

## Weapon: Vibroblade
### Data Extract Profile
[[File:Vibroblade TPHelm.png|thumb|right|Vibroblade]]

==Description==
A '''''Vibroblade''''' is a melee weapon used across the Inner Sphere, first introduced in [[2400]] by the [[Free Worlds League]]. The vibroblade is used (and most often shaped) like a conventional sword, but also includes a power pack in the hilt for a series of motor systems. When activated, the vibroblade's cutting edge oscillates at a very high speed, cutting through anything in its path.

Vibroblades are available in a variety of sizes, ranging from personal weapons to [[Experimental Technology|experimental]] BattleMech sized versions.

==Personal Vibroblades==
* ''Vibroblade'' - The vibroblade is approximately the size of a dagger or knife.<ref name=MW3Ep134>''MechWarrior, Third Edition'', p. 134</ref>
* [[Vibrosword]] - Very popular with the few people who can use it, the vibrosword is a typical cutlass, rapier, or broadsword shaped weapon.<ref name=MW3Ep134 />

===Related Weapons===
* [[Vibrokatana]] - First developed for the [[Draconis Combine]]'s [[DEST]] teams, the vibrokatana is very effective against [[battle armor]].<ref name=LTMEGp26>''LosTech: The MechWarrior Equipment Guide'', p. 26</ref>
* [[Vibroaxe]] - The vibroaxe was originally developed for utility work like logging or fire rescue. As it isn't designed for combat it imposes a -1 to hit penalty.<ref name=LTMEGp26 />
* [[Vibromace]] - The vibromace was developed in the [[Circinus Federation]] for their blood sports. Instead of using the vibrations to slice, the head of the vibromace uses them to pulverize whatever it hits. The cruder manufacturing capability of the Federation result in a -1 to hit penalty.

===Related Equipment===
* [[Vibro Lock Pick Kit]] - A usual tool amongst intelligence personnel, which utilizes a just 7.5 cm blade to cut through locks instead of picking them.

==BattleMech Vibroblades==
The BattleMech Vibroblade was developed by [[Draconis Combine]] engineers in [[3059]] but didn't reach the deployment until [[3065]].<ref name=TOp292>''Tactical Operations'', p. 292</ref> Using their [[Sword (weapon)|Sword]] as a foundation, they were able to incorporate the same technology that makes the personal vibroblade and vibrokatana so effective. Unlike the standard Sword the BattleMech vibroblade comes in three sizes: small, medium, and large. When unpowered, the vibroblade acts as a standard 'Mech sized sword. When the pilot activates the vibroblade, and successfully attacks an opponent, the cutting edge slices even deeper into the target, inflicting more damage than a sword. The tradeoff for this increased damage is the heat generated by the vibroblade's mechanisms and the fact that an active vibroblade won't benefit from [[triple-strength myomer]].<ref name=TOp292 />

Though the vibroblade offers some advantages over the standard Sword, it hasn't yet been deployed in large numbers. In fact it's only been seen in the arenas of [[Solaris]] and other game worlds<ref name=TR3052Up162>''Technical Readout: 3055 Upgrade'', p. 162</ref>, barring its usage by [[The Society]] in the ''[[Osteon]]'''s Configuration B during the [[Wars of Reaving]].

==Description==
The '''Wrecking Ball''' is a piece of construction equipment that is rarely seen on the battlefield. Though it has been used by the arena fighters on [[Solaris]], the lack of range and interference with other equipment mean it's not popular with the regular armies of the Inner Sphere. Instead the Wrecking Ball is usually found on [[IndustrialMech]]s and support vehicles that are used for demolition.

Essentially nothing more than a massive rock at the end of a chain, the Wrecking Ball uses its weight and momentum to build up energy during a swing. This energy is then imparted to the targeted building, causing it to collapse. Occasionally this item is used in combat (as a less effective version of the [[Flail]]) by ConstructionMechs and DemolitionMechs that have been pressed into emergency service. In addition to the lack of range a Wrecking Ball is inherently difficult to control and can actually inflict damage on the unit mounting it, either by a reversed blow or by pulling the unit off balance and causing it to fall. In any event, the Wrecking Ball is better suited for demolition than combat.

==Rules==
<div class="gamerules">
===Game Rules===
A Wrecking Ball inflicts 8 damage on any target it hits, or 8+4d6 versus buildings.<ref name="tw143"/>

In gameplay the Wrecking Ball imposes a +1 to hit modifier. In addition if the player rolls a 2 on the to-hit roll, the Wrecking Ball misses the target and slams into the attacking unit itself. This self-inflicted attack causes half the normal damage (4 points) and is allocated against the Front Hit Location Table. After this damage is allocated, the controlling player must make a successful Piloting skill roll or the wildly moving Wrecking Ball causes the 'Mech to fall, inflicting normal falling damage.<ref name="tw146"/>

A Support Vehicle carrying a Wrecking Ball can make a single physical attack on any target in the Wrecking Ball's arc. This attack incurs a +2 to-hit penalty in addition to the standard movement and terrain modifiers. If successful, the Wrecking Ball inflicts full damage. 'Mechs hit by the Wrecking Ball use the standard hit location table, and must make a successful Piloting roll as if the 'Mech had been charged.<ref name="tw143">''Total Warfare'', p. 143</ref>

Bipedal 'Mechs can carry a Wrecking Ball in each arm, but must remove the hand actuator to do so. Quad 'Mechs cannot mount a wrecking ball. A Wrecking Ball may not be mounted in any area that mounts a [[Chainsaw]], [[Dual Saw]], [[Pile Driver]], [[Mining Drill]], [[Rock Cutter]], [[Combine (Equipment)|Combine]], or [[Backhoe]].<ref name="tm249">''TechManual'', p. 249</ref>

Support Vehicles can only carry a Wrecking Ball in the Turret location, and only if none of the prohibited construction equipment types listed above are already present.<ref name="tm249"/>
